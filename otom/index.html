An idea, in progress

Because every tail deserves a meal.

There are millions of street animals in Egypt and nobody counts them. One Tail One Meal wants to feed them. It doesn't yet — not a single meal. What it has is a first real step, and it's already working: a free map of where the hungry tails actually are.

See the first step What the idea is

No van yet. No staff. No income. Just a map, and whoever wants to help fill it.

Where this comes from

One person, feeling useless about it.

“My partner & I have always been concerned about the welfare of stray animals, and we often discuss how to feed and care for hungry tails.”
“I am an Egyptian-Australian who always feels helpless when thinking of all the forgotten dogs and cats in Egypt.”

That is the whole origin. Not an organisation with a history — one person on the other side of the world who couldn't stop thinking about it, and decided to build the smallest useful thing rather than wait until the big one was affordable.

The idea

A kitchen on wheels that turns waste into meals.

Butchers throw away meat and bone every day. Street animals go hungry every day. The idea is a van that closes the gap between those two sentences.

Collect

Stop at local butchers and take the leftover chicken, beef and bones that would otherwise be binned.

Cook

Cook it safely in a built-in cooker on board, so what gets served is clean, warm food rather than scraps.

Serve

Serve it on eco-friendly plates made from vegetables — edible, so nothing is left behind on the street.

None of this exists yet. There is no van, no cooker, no route and no team. Saying otherwise would be the easiest way to lose the only thing this has, which is that everything it tells you is true.

Why a map and not a van

The map is the cheap version of the van.

Buy a vehicle, fit a kitchen, hire someone to drive it — and only then find out which streets actually need it. Or find out first, for the price of a phone bill, using the people who are already out there feeding.

What a van costs to guess with

A vehicle, a permit, a cooker, fuel, and a wage — before a single meal is served, and before anyone knows whether the route is the right one. The published goal for that first truck is $8,000.

What the map costs to learn with

About $36 a month in servers and map calls. One vet visit. If the map fills in, the van has a reason and a route. If it doesn't, nothing was spent finding that out.

This is the order, and it isn't going to be reversed: results first, then the ask. Money raised against an idea is money you can't raise later against a result.

The first step — and it's real

Spot a Paw.

A free app and a map for the street animals of Egypt. Anyone who sees a dog or a cat in trouble drops a pin in seconds — a photo, a place, what's wrong. Rescuers, vets and shelters nearby see it immediately and can say “I'm on my way.”

This is the part that isn't a plan. It's built, it's in Arabic and English, and you can use it today.

  • A case that doesn't scroll away. A Facebook post is gone in an hour. A pin stays until somebody marks it rescued.
  • Feeding spots. The places people already feed, mapped — so a round can be planned instead of remembered.
  • Every animal is followed home. Spotted, on the way, rescued, adopted.

How to help

In the order that actually helps.

The first two are free and matter more than the third. That is not modesty — a map nobody answers is worse than no map at all.

01

Put a paw on the map

If you're in Cairo and you see an animal that needs help, drop a pin. It takes seconds and it costs nothing. Every pin makes the next person's map less empty.

Get the app

02

Answer one

This is the one that's genuinely needed. If you already rescue, foster, run a shelter, practise as a vet, or feed a street every evening — ask for a Rescuer or Shelter account and be the person on the other end of a pin.

Ask for an account

03

Give time

“Volunteering your time at One Tail One Meal is a rewarding experience. Help us with ideation, execution and reaching people.” Hosting a food drive counts too.

Say hello

And money — honestly.

There is a GoFundMe, and its goal is $8,000: permits, the first food truck, and a wage for a local animal lover to drive it. It's real and it's open. But it's for the van, and the van comes second. Right now the running cost of the map is covered personally, and the most useful thing anyone can give is a pin or an answer, not a pound.
