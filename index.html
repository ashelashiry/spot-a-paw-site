Why we exist

A hungry animal becomes desperate. A fed animal becomes calm, gentle, and hopeful.

Spot a Paw puts the people who already care — the neighbour with a bag of kibble, the driver with a spare hour, the vet who'll take one more — on the same map, at the same time.

Our mission

Get every stray the help it needs — food, water, medical care, a rescue, a home — by connecting the people who spot them with the people who can act. No call centre, no waiting list. A pin on a map and a neighbour who cares.

Our vision

A world where no paw goes unseen. Every neighbourhood with its own rescuers, feeders and drivers; every feeding spot on a route; every rescue a story someone can celebrate. Starting in Cairo and Sydney — built to work anywhere there's a stray and a phone.

Kindness in action

Compassion is a verb. The app exists to turn "someone should do something" into "I'm on my way".

Community first

Karma, not gamification. Every helper is named, thanked and counted on their own rescue record.

No waste, no waiting

Leftover food, spare seats, spare minutes — routed to a tail that needs them tonight.

Dignity for every tail

Feeding restores trust. A calm, fed animal is one that can be caught, treated, sterilised and rehomed.

The mothership

Spot a Paw is the first thing One Tail One Meal has built.

"Because every tail deserves a meal."

One Tail One Meal is an idea, not an organisation — one person who couldn't stop thinking about the forgotten dogs and cats of Egypt. The idea is food trucks driven by local heroes: collect the leftover meat butchers throw out, cook it safely on board, and serve it warm on edible plates made from vegetables. No waste, no pollution, no empty bellies.

None of that exists yet — no van, no team, no income. This app is the cheap version of it: find out where the need actually is, for the price of a phone bill, before anyone buys a vehicle to guess with.

Cairo first. Then Alexandria, Giza, and anywhere a tail is hungry. Spot a Paw is how the trucks — if they are ever built — will know where the tails are.

How it works

Eight ways to help, starting the moment you see a paw.

Pick your role. Some people spot, some feed, some drive. Spot a Paw lets you do one, or all of them.

Drop a pin

Spot a paw in need? Mark their location on the map in seconds — a photo or a short video, what kind of animal, what they need.

Report status

Tell others whether they need food, water, medical help, shelter — or that it's a mum with pups. The feed and the map update for everyone nearby.

Save a paw

Tap Help this Paw. Team up, share updates, mark it rescued — and it joins the Success Stories, with karma to everyone who helped.

Plan a feeding route

Chain your feeding spots into tonight's round. Numbered stops, arrows on the map, "Fed today" check-ins so the next feeder knows.

Drive

Got wheels? Carry a paw to the vet or a shelter when someone nearby needs a lift. Later, the same routes carry food: a driver running a feeding round, and one day a truck cooking on board. The map is the same either way.

Treat a paw

We are asking vets to join and offer what they can — a consultation, a sterilisation, a wound seen to before it turns septic. Pro bono where it is possible, discounted where it is not. A vet on the map is the difference between a report and a recovery.

Take one in

Shelters are the hardest link in the chain and the one everybody assumes exists. We are asking them to join so a rescuer can see, before they pick an animal up, whether there is anywhere for it to go.

Share the offcuts

Butchers bin bone and trim every day while animals go hungry on the same street. We want local butchers registered so that food is offered instead of thrown out — and, once there is money behind it, so we can buy proper cuts from them rather than only taking what is left.

The other half

A map is only as good as the people reading it.

Anyone can drop a pin. That part is easy, and on its own it is worth nothing — a report nobody answers is just a photograph of something sad. The whole value of Spot a Paw comes from the second person: the vet three streets away, the shelter with one space left, the driver who happens to be going that way.

So the app is not really a map. It is a way of finding the people who were already helping, and putting them within reach of each other.

Six kinds of help

A community carries a badge, because who you are changes what you can offer. Rescuers go and get them. Feeders keep the colonies alive between crises. Vets treat and sterilise. Shelters take them in. Drivers close the distance. And butchers — the newest one — have the thing every feeder needs.

Near you, or anywhere

Every community belongs to a country, so you can look at just the ones where you live or at all of them at once. A street-level group for your own neighbourhood works exactly the same as a national network of clinics — the difference is only who it is for.

Reaching everyone at once

The person who runs a community can send a message to every member's phone. Deliberately only them: a loudspeaker anyone can pick up is a loudspeaker everybody mutes. It is for the things that cannot wait — a dog in the road, a van leaving in an hour.

Waste on one side, hunger on the other

The butcher who has meat, and the feeder who needs it

Butchers throw away bone and offcuts every single day. Street animals go hungry the same day, often on the same street. The two have never had a way to find each other, and the gap between them is measured in metres.

A butcher community closes it. Somebody posts “8 kg of offcuts, ready until 6pm” and everyone in that community gets it on their phone, with directions. The offer expires on its own, so nobody drives across town for meat that went an hour ago.

And it works before a single butcher installs anything — whoever runs the community can post on their behalf when the message arrives by WhatsApp, which is how most of this actually happens.

If you already rescue, foster, treat, drive or feed — you are the point of this. Start a community for your street, your clinic or your city, and be the person on the other end of somebody's first pin.

Inside the app

Built like a rescue tool, dressed like a story.

Nile green, papyrus ivory, and a little Egyptian art — because the first tails we're feeding are in Cairo.

Paws & Tails

The feed: every paw spotted near you

Three tabs — Spotted Paws, Feeding Spots, Success Stories. Filter by what they need, by distance, by animal. A counter tells you exactly how many paws need help within your radius.

  • Needs help / Help underway status at a glance
  • Photo or video on every card, with a mini-map
  • One tap to Help this Paw
Rescue Map

See it all on one map

Spotted paws, feeding spots, tonight's routes with numbered stops and arrows, and the vets and shelters within your search radius. Every pin opens straight into the report.

  • Colour-coded routes when you run more than one
  • Vets & Shelters nearby, re-searched as you move
  • Filter by urgency
Spot a Paw

Thirty seconds from sighting to pin

Snap a photo or a short video, tap what they need, drop the pin where they are (or let your location do it). That's a report. Everyone nearby sees it.

  • Dog, cat or other — and mums with pups
  • Food, water, medical, rescue, shelter
  • Edit or withdraw your own reports any time
Success Stories

Rescued. Adopted. Microchipped. Sterilised.

Every paw that makes it home is celebrated by name, with the people who helped. The four tabs are the four milestones a stray's life can reach.

  • Rescued and adopted stories with photos
  • Microchipped and sterilised records — the long game
  • Karma to every helper on the story
My Rescues & Profile

Your record, your cartouche

Every paw you've spotted, helped or rescued, in one place. Your profile medallion carries your name in a cartouche — paws helped, reports made, karma, days active, sterilised and microchipped underneath.

  • Spotted · Rescued · Helping · Withdrawn tabs
  • Choose your cartouche name and photo
  • Six looks: Egyptian, Standard and four regional skins — by location or by taste
  • Communities: join local rescue groups
Look & feel

Six looks. One rescue map.

Spot a Paw was born in Cairo, so it wears papyrus and Nile scenes by default. Pick the look of your region — or simply the one you like — in Profile → Look & feel. Scenery, papers and icons change; the buttons, your data and your language never do.
